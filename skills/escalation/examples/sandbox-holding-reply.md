## Situation

The <region> sales VP has a long action list for an AI sandbox for <customer>, a large customer in <region>.
Note A goes out first. The VP answers it <n> minutes later and asks for answers "today".
Yu-Kuan is in all-day offsite workshops. Note B buys time until the real answer.

## Sent

### A. <date>

`````text
Hey <sales VP>,

We’re on track. Some of these action items are a bit overwrought and not needed (as <security lead> mentioned any security questions should be answered by our standard security questionnaires, roadmap timing should be handled by existing roadmaps etc). The important things like the site provisioning, product timelines (will be part of roadmap) and most important exciting demos of the latest AI are all on track.

YL

________________________________
`````

### B. <date>

`````text
Hey <sales VP>, we’ll absolutely have the best experience possible for <customer> - I just spent an hour planning w/ my team yesterday on how we might demo to <customer>, who’s going to do what, etc so we’ll record that out. Thanks for your patience as we’ve all been in all day workshops at the offsite.

You’ll have all the info you need by EOD.
`````

## Why it works

- He opens with "We're on track" before any detail. It did not stop the follow-up ask, so note B was still needed.
- He trims the ask. He calls some action items "overwrought and not needed" and names who already owns them.
- He names the three things that matter, so the asker sees the important work is covered.
- The second note gives proof of effort ("an hour planning w/ my team") and the reason for the delay.
- He closes with one firm time promise: "by EOD". There is no apology and no detail.
- The sign-off is "YL" or nothing. Short notes stay informal.
