## Situation

## Sent

### <date> (same chat, <n> minutes later)

`````text
However, <sales lead> what I struggle to understand is even if web search is technically "on", if they or any user kept their AGAI queries fairly scoped, e.g. "look for xyz trends from latest board decks, etc" then AGAI in most cases is smart enough to only pull from or prioritize from board decks first. So whether web search is on vs off is irrelevant. 

 

Even if it had looked at web search, because everything is cited clearly, they can just ignore anything that has a web citation. Why can't they just start testing the product this way? There's no reason to throw out the baby with the bathwater. They can start testing the product now and probably cover <n>% of the cases they want to test. Have we tried being more adaptable & flexible in how we approach the customer? I don't know what <example> or <example 2> refers too, but if they don't go searching for <example> or <example 2> they're not going to find it. 

 

The above isn't saying we don't ship web search controls. Of course we will and are working asap on it, and recognize we will have it for when they want to turn it on officially. But we'd also appreciate your partnership in looking for some of these creative workarounds w/ the customer as well. Cheers.
`````

## Why it works

- He opens with "However" and names the person. The push-back is direct and in the open chat.
- He gives a product reason: scoped questions pull from board decks first, and every web source is cited.
- He uses a plain idiom ("throw out the baby with the bathwater") and a rough number ("<n>% of the cases").
- He asks the field a pointed question: "Have we tried being more adaptable & flexible...?"
- He restates the commitment ("Of course we will") so the push-back does not read as a refusal.
- Caution: the field pushed back hard, because the bad results were real. Use this move only when the product truly covers the case.
