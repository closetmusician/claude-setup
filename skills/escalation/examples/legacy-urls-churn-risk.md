## Situation

A CS leader flags a churn risk. Deactivated legacy URLs will break thousands of embedded files for a <$> customer.
<eng lead>, who knew about it, is on PTO.

## Sent

### <date>

`````text
Hi <CS lead>,

Thanks for letting us know - unfortunately I wasn’t aware of this prior to <eng lead>’s PTO, @<engineer> have you had a chance to connect w/ <CS lead> yet? If not, would you mind connecting w/ him and exploring what options we have available? Normally I would think we’d handle this with redirects, not sure why it’s not viable not. Also adding @<engineer 2> who might be able to help brainstorm solutions if we get stuck? Once you explore options if you can share back w/ BE leads, let’s discuss then share w/ <CS lead>’s team to see what we take and how we get back to the customer?

Thanks,
YL
`````

## Why it works

- He opens with thanks and an honest note that he was not aware.
- He asks <engineer> a direct question, then asks him to connect with the CS leader.
- He offers a likely fix ("redirects") as a question, not an order.
- He adds a second engineer as backup for ideas.
- He sets the loop: explore options, share with leads, then go back to the customer.
- It is one paragraph, and it makes no promise to the customer yet.
