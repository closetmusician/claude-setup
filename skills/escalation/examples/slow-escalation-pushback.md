# slow-escalation-pushback

```yaml
audience: CSM
reply_type: push-back
channel: mail

```

## Situation

The CFO sees a discount request and asks why a customer could not load board books for <n>+ months.
A CS manager says the case was escalated through Support. The CFO asks Yu-Kuan and the VP of Engineering for more insight.

## Sent

### <date>

`````text
+ @<VP eng>

Hi <CSM>, I’m surprised to hear this as well - was this escalated earlier? I noticed that the customer has also never archived their mtgs & books, and with many books over <n>+ docs each it’s more likely they’ll hit this problem. Have they taken our workaround advice to start archiving their books & mtgs?

For this specifically, we have established processes for especially serious T2 tickets (this certainly would’ve qualified) to short-circuit escalations that cuts through the queue. Could you please ask <support lead> or whoever to assist with the escalation process to expedite and cut through the queue? Mean while @<VP eng> let’s have the team look into the T2 ticket asap? Thanks all!

YL
`````

## Why it works

- He adds the VP of Engineering first ("+ @<VP eng>"). Action shows before words.
- He answers the CSM with a question: "was this escalated earlier?" It points the delay at the process.
- He names a customer habit that causes the issue: books with <n>+ documents that are never archived.
- He points to the existing T2 escalation path and says this case "certainly would've qualified".
- He gives engineering a one-line ask and closes "Thanks all!".
- Caution: the next day the CFO called the answers "half baked" and asked for a root cause. A CFO wants the cause, not only the process.
