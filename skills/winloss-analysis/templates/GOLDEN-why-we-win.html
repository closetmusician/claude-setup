We win where we already are. Expansion (60%) and referral/director-familiarity (59%) are the two largest themes because the winning motion is farming the installed base and the boardroom network — a durable strength, not a confession: it converts to low churn and a director network that keeps compounding.2 The platform lever is the strongest measurable edge: deals sold on the Acme One Platform win at 46.4% versus 10.4% for non-platform deals — a Finance-data fact, and the single largest win lever in the book.2 Governed AI is a genuine "why now": it opens Enterprise upgrades and, at Newcastle, closed a real security concern about staff feeding board papers into shadow Copilot; buyers still pay for the platform and licensing rather than the AI line itself, so credit the AI pull and note the licensing purchase in the same breath.5, 33 Named-competitor takeaways are rare (~6 of 88) because new-logo growth comes from SMB first-board greenfield, not from beating rivals head-to-head.5, 2 Themes overlap — a deal can be both an expansion and an AI hook — so the percentages are not mutually exclusive.

Themes overlap — a deal can be both an expansion and an AI hook — so percentages are of the 38-win base and do not sum to 100%. The Salesforce win-reason field is blank on about 54% of AMER wins, so themes are reconstructed from transcripts and CRM notes, not the picklist.5

Expansion beats new logos. 21 of 38 wins (55%) are upsells to accounts that already own Boards. On Global Payments, the seller's own words were to pull the Worldpay entities "under the global payments subscription."6 The "brand" is really the boardroom network: about 26% win because directors already use Acme elsewhere, so the picklist "Brand Strength" is a proxy for that pull, not abstract reputation.7, 5 Named-rival takeaways are rare (~11%) — only United Rentals (BoardVantage) and Etsy (OnBoard) are clean, evidenced portal displacements, and "Brainloop" competitor tags are a SKU-catalog artifact, because Brainloop is Acme-owned, not takeaways.5

Canada wins the same way as the US, at lower volume. Of 38 real AMER wins, about 33 are US and only ~5 are Canada (Emera, General Fusion, Talon Metals, Associated Engineering, RFA), and the win rate is US 27.5% versus Canada 16.0% all-sizes (21.2% versus 10.9% Finance-clean).1 The mechanism is identical across the border — installed-base expansion (Emera, Associated Engineering, RFA), director familiarity and single sign-on (Talon), and product fit (General Fusion). No Canada-specific competitor or differentiator surfaced, so the gap is one of volume, not of why. One honest counter-signal on AI: the Associated Engineering board "prefers to rely on their own critical thinking rather than utilizing AI tools," so the GovernAI hook that drives US Enterprise upgrades did not travel north — the deal still closed on the installed-base expansion motion.18

APAC — the amendment correction, referral, and phantom competition

Why the raw win rate misleads. APAC's raw 42.9% looks like the strongest region, but 31 of 54 raw wins are sub-$5K amendments (avg ~$1,650, total $51,125) — contract tweaks on customers who had already decided to stay. On the Finance-clean basis the region is 23W / 63L = 26.7%, in line with AMER and EMEA. Largest amendments: Cue Energy $4,845, Cruising Yacht Club $4,738, Murrumbidgee Irrigation $4,622.4

The durable finding is referral conversion. Referral converts at ~67.9% in APAC vs 44.3% in AMER and 46.6% in EMEA2 — referred existing customers expand and renew with little friction. But read it on the clean basis: 26 of the 31 excluded amendments (84%) are referral-sourced, so the raw referral lift is inflated by the same low-value tweaks. Among the 23 real wins, referral is 10 (43%), tied with outbound (10); marketing is 3.4

Deliberate low-tier selling. 18 of 23 real wins (78%) are the lowest tiers — Essential, Foundations, Starter; only 4 are Pro. Median real-win ARR is about $9,585; only Swinburne ($38K) and NZ Bioeconomy ($29K) clear $20K. APAC lands small, at the entry packages, and expands later.4

Phantom competition — only one named rival was displaced all month. The Salesforce competitor field is blank across APAC, and the only named-competitor displacement in the evidence is BoardEffect at BlueCHP — a Acme-owned product, so an internal migration decided on the AI roadmap, not an external conquest.38 Swinburne's win note also frames the choice against BoardEffect.42 APAC's competitive strength is over-stated by both the amendment inflation and the near-total absence of real head-to-head wins.
